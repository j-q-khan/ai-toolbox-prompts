# Claude build prompt: Claude Design System Tutorial: Make Every Design Match Your Brand

Part 6 of the Claude Design series. Copy everything below the line into a new chat with Claude.
It follows Anthropic's official Claude Design pages (checked 2026-11-15). Use only made-up or your own non-confidential details, never a client's or employer's files, and check everything Claude makes before you rely on it.

---

Paste this into a new chat with Claude on a paid plan (design systems are a beta on Pro, Max, Ultra, Team and Enterprise, not Free). Attach the brand kit file from this folder, or paste it under the prompt. Use a made-up brand like this one, never a client's or employer's files, and never ask Claude to copy a real company's brand.

Goal: set up a small design system from my brand kit, then test it with one design, every piece marked as a draft.
My brand kit (made up; Pebble Lane Bakery is not a real business; replace it with your own non-confidential brand kit):
- Logo: a round mark with one wheat stalk, plain shapes only, with the name Pebble Lane Bakery.
- Colours: flour cream #F5EBDD (background), crust brown #8A5A2B (text and buttons), sage green #8FA382 (accents).
- Fonts: one warm, rounded font for headings, and one plain, easy-to-read font for body text.
- Components: a Primary Button (crust brown, cream text, rounded corners) and a Card (cream, thin sage border).
- Layout: an 8-pixel spacing scale and plenty of white space.
Steps, in order:
1. If anything in my brand kit is unclear, ask me up to two questions first, and wait for my answers.
2. Build a design system from this brand kit: the colours with their colour codes and roles, the two fonts with sizes for headings and body text, the Primary Button and the Card, and the spacing rule.
3. Check that every text colour has good contrast against its background, and tell me any pairing that doesn't.
4. Wait for me to say it looks right.
5. Then make one test design with the design system: a one-pager announcing the bakery's weekend opening hours, using placeholder hours in square brackets.
6. List anything in the test design that doesn't follow the design system.
7. Use only the facts in my brand kit. Don't invent a slogan, prices, awards, reviews or names.
8. Put the word "Draft" on the design system and on the test design.
9. Don't copy or imitate any real company's logo, brand, colours or lettering, and use no photos of real people.
Output: the design system marked as a draft, one test one-pager marked as a draft, and a short list of anything that didn't match.
