Paste this into a new chat with Claude. Claude Design works on any plan, Free included. First click Output in the message box and choose Design. Use a made-up brief like this one, never a client's or employer's files, and never ask for something like a real company's logo.

Goal: draft a simple logo and a one-page brand kit in Claude Design from my brief, every piece marked as a draft.
My brief (made up; Pebble Lane Bakery is not a real business; replace it with your own non-confidential brief):
- Make: a logo for Pebble Lane Bakery, a small neighbourhood bakery.
- Mark: a round mark with one wheat stalk, plain shapes only.
- Name: Pebble Lane Bakery, in a warm, rounded font.
- Colours: flour cream #F5EBDD, crust brown #8A5A2B, sage green #8FA382.
- Fonts: one warm, rounded font for the name and headings, and one plain, easy-to-read font for body text.
- Where it goes: a shop sign, a paper bag and the top of a website.
Steps, in order:
1. If anything in my brief is unclear, ask me up to two questions first, and wait for my answers.
2. Make three different logo ideas side by side on one artboard, using only text and plain shapes in my three colours.
3. Wait for me to pick one.
4. Make a second artboard: a one-page brand kit with the chosen logo at the top, the three colours as swatches with their colour codes, the two fonts with one sample line each, and one line on where the logo shouldn't go (for example, over a busy photo).
5. Check that every text colour on the brand kit has good contrast against its background, and tell me any pairing that doesn't.
6. Use only the facts in my brief. Don't invent a slogan, prices, awards, reviews or names.
7. Put the word "Draft" on every artboard.
8. Don't copy or imitate any real company's logo, brand, mascot or lettering, and use no photos of real people.
9. At the end, remind me in two lines: Anthropic's terms assign me its rights in outputs only "if any" exist, a logo made by A.I. may not be protected by copyright on its own, and trademark is a different law, so I should check trademark as its own step and check with a lawyer for my situation before using the logo in business.
Output: three logo drafts, one brand kit page marked as a draft, and the two-line reminder.
