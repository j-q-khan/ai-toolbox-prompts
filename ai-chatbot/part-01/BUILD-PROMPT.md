It turns your own website pages into the questions and answers a chatbot should handle, plus a first message that tells visitors it's an AI. Try it first with the made-up Test Bakery details in the prompt, then swap in your own public pages. Don't paste anything private: no passwords, customer details or staff names. Check every answer it writes against your real pages before you put it in a chatbot. Written from the makers' docs, not run.

I run a small business and I'm setting up a chatbot for my website. Below are my public web pages, pasted as text. Use only what these pages say.

[PASTE YOUR PAGES HERE. To try it first, use this made-up example: Test Bakery, 1 Example Street. Open Tuesday to Saturday, 8am to 4pm, closed Sunday and Monday. We deliver within 5 miles for orders over $30. Custom cakes need 3 days' notice. Contact: test@example.com or +1 555 0100.]

Do these four things:
1. List the 15 questions a customer is most likely to ask this business, each with an answer of 2 short sentences at most, written only from my pages. Format each one as "Q:" then the question, and "A:" then the answer.
2. After the list, add a section called "Not in your pages" with up to 5 common customer questions my pages don't answer, so I can add that information or have the chatbot hand those questions to a person.
3. Write one first message for the chatbot, under 40 words, that says it's an AI assistant and not a person, says it answers from the business's website, and gives the way to reach a person from my pages.
4. Write one fallback reply, under 30 words, for when the chatbot doesn't know the answer: it says so plainly and gives the way to reach a person.
Rules: never invent prices, hours, addresses or policies; if my pages don't say it, put it under "Not in your pages". Use plain, friendly English.
