## LLM (large language model)
A model that writes by predicting the next token, adding it on, and predicting again.
Anthropic: "AI language models with many parameters" that are "trained on vast amounts of text data".
Google: "LLMs are essentially autocomplete mechanisms that can automatically predict (complete) thousands of tokens."

## Token
The small piece of text a model works in: a whole word, part of a word, a single character or a byte.
Anthropic: tokens "can correspond to words, subwords, characters, or even bytes".
Anthropic: "For Claude, the number of characters a token represents depends on the model and the language."
Anthropic: on earlier Claude models a token was about 3.5 English characters; Claude 4.7 and later use a newer tokenizer that makes about 30 percent more tokens for the same text.

## Parameter
One of the numbers a model learns during training; a large model has billions of them.
Google: "The weights and biases that a model learns during training."
Google: "Transformers contain hundreds of billion or even trillions of parameters."

## Training
The learning stage that happens before you ever type to the model. How it works is Part 2 of the series.

## Transformer and self-attention
The Transformer is the design behind these models, from the 2017 paper Attention Is All You Need.
Google calls it "the most successful and widely used architecture for building LLMs".
Self-attention, in Google's words, asks for each token: "How much does each other token of input affect the interpretation of this token?"

## Context window
The text a model can see at once, measured in tokens, including its own reply.
Anthropic: a "working memory" for the model, different from what it learned in training.
Anthropic: Claude's newest models have a 1M-token context window; other Claude models, such as Claude Sonnet 4.5 (deprecated), 200k (checked 2026-11-23).

## Context rot
The model gets worse at finding and using details as the context window fills.
Anthropic: "As token count grows, accuracy and recall degrade, a phenomenon known as context rot."
What helps: start a fresh chat with a short summary of what matters.

## Temperature
A setting for how much randomness goes into picking the next token.
Anthropic: "a parameter that controls the randomness of a model's predictions during text generation."
Anthropic: "Even with temperature set to 0, the results will not be fully deterministic".

## Hallucination
An answer that sounds right and is false.
OpenAI: "Hallucinations are plausible but false statements generated by language models."
OpenAI: training and evaluation "reward guessing over acknowledging uncertainty".
What helps: check names, numbers, dates and quotes somewhere you trust. Add the line: If you're not sure, say you don't know.
Anthropic: "Explicitly give Claude permission to admit uncertainty."

## Agent
A system built around a model that chooses its own next steps and uses tools, such as web search.
Anthropic: agents are "systems where LLMs dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage".
OpenAI: "Agents are systems that independently accomplish tasks on your behalf." Simple chatbots are not agents.

## Generative AI
The part of AI that makes new text, pictures or sound.
Google: "An emerging transformative field with no formal definition."
