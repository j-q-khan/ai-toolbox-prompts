# Kling 4.0 settings sheet: text to video, image to video, Motion Control (facts checked 2026-11-23)

To keep a copy: paste this into Notepad and save it with the box called Save as type set to All files, as kling-settings-sheet.md. On a Mac: open TextEdit, choose Format, then Make Plain Text, before you paste and save.

Every quote below is from Kling's own pages on 2026-11-23. Nothing was signed up for, run or generated. Kling's guide says features are "subject to the actual product interface": check the screen on the day.

## Where things are (Kling's home page)
- [ ] At the bottom of Kling's home page, under "Creative Studio", then "Video Generation": the workspace (kling.ai/app). The links of the same name in the top menus open feature pages.
- [ ] Two ways in: "Omni Reference" (the default: words alone, or pictures and videos) and "Keyframes" (your own picture as the first frame, or first and last frames). They can't be combined in one generation.
- [ ] "Motion Control": under the same "Creative Studio" heading, next to "Video Generation".

## The models (Kling 4.0 user guide, Nov 4, 2026)
- [ ] Kling 4.0: "3-30s"; 720p, 1080p or 4K.
- [ ] Kling 4.0 Flash: "3–20 seconds", "720p"; "does not currently support start-and-last frames or multiple keyframes".

## The settings and their defaults
- [ ] Duration: "3–30 seconds, adjustable in 1-second increments; default: 10 seconds". Test at 5.
- [ ] Aspect ratio: "21:9 / 16:9 / 1:1 / 9:16 / Auto; default: Auto". 9:16 for a reel or a short, 16:9 for a normal YouTube video. In Keyframes: "After uploading images, the aspect ratio follows the first keyframe." Crop your photo first.
- [ ] Resolution: 720p, 1080p or 4K. Kling's credit guide (Jul 28, 2026) lists "1080p Video Generation" on its paid plans, not on Basic. Test at 720p.
- [ ] Sound: "enabled by default and have no separate toggles". Put the sound you want in the prompt.
- [ ] Multi-shot: "Multi-shot is enabled by default". For one product shot, end the prompt with: one continuous shot, no cuts.
- [ ] Credits: no Kling page we could open lists what a Kling 4.0 clip costs. Check your balance before and after a test.

## The rules for a prompt (Kling 4.0 user guide)
- [ ] "Use descriptive language and minimize abstract terms and negative instructions."
- [ ] Not "Premium look". Instead: "Side backlighting outlines the object's edges, the background is blurred, and fine matte grain is visible on the surface."
- [ ] "Only two types of negative instructions are truly necessary: no subtitles and no background music." The model "may add these by default". The guide's own examples also ask for "no cuts", so keep that line too.
- [ ] "Start with one sentence that clearly describes the element, scene, action, and emotion. Then add only the details that meaningfully affect the result".

## Image to video (Keyframes, your photo as the first frame)
- [ ] The guide's wording: "Use @Image1 as the first frame, then describe what happens next."
- [ ] Describe only what moves. Kling's older image to video guide (Nov 24, 2025): "A description that significantly deviates from the image may cause a camera cut or transition".
Use @Image1 as the first frame. Steam rises slowly from the mug, and the camera pushes in gently, one continuous shot, no cuts, no subtitles, no background music.
- [ ] First and last frames (Kling 4.0, not Flash): "Use @Image1 as the first frame and @Image2 as the last frame, then describe the [process] in between."
- [ ] Check: pause on the first second next to your photo. Shape, colour, details. If they drift, cut the prompt back to one movement.

## Motion Control (Motion Control user guide, Mar 5, 2026)
- [ ] What it's for: "precise control of a character's movements and facial expressions based on a reference image." "Real human actions are recommended". Kling built it for copying a person's movement from a video.
- [ ] Before you upload: Kling's terms (2026/04/21, 4.3) say you "hold the intellectual property rights to the Input or … have obtained legal authorization from the relevant owner". Never upload someone else's video without their permission. The terms also say you may not "impersonate any person or entity" (3.1), and Kling takes complaints over "portrait rights" (6.1).
- [ ] Step 1 (the guide's VIDEO 2.6 steps): "Add video of character actions to mimic; you can upload video from local resources or choose from the Motion Library."
- [ ] Your own video: "Upload a single character motion reference." "Ensure the character's entire body and head are clearly visible and not obstructed." "The action video must be a single continuous shot". "Please avoid cuts, shot changes, or camera movements". "Avoid overly fast motions; steady, moderate movements yield the best results." "3–30 seconds, and the generated video duration will match the length of the uploaded video."
- [ ] Step 2: "Add the character image, ensuring the proportions match those in the video for optimal results." Whole body in the video, whole body in the picture.
- [ ] Step 3: "By default, the video will be generated through "Character Orientation Matches Video"." Leave it on for a first try.
- [ ] Step 4: "Enter the prompt to control background elements and other information". Then start the generation. If the clip comes back shorter than your video, slow the movement down.
- [ ] Cost, when checked: Kling VIDEO 3.0 Motion Control "12 Credits/s" (Professional), "9 Credits/s" (Standard).
- [ ] Before you spend: if the output is shorter than your upload, "the consumed credits cannot be refunded."
