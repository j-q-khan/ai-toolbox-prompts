# Claude build prompt: Gemini vs ChatGPT: Which One Should You Pay For in 2026?

Part 4 of the Google AI Plans series. The prompt is the script's own "Claude build prompt" section, word for word.

---

Paste this into Claude, in a new chat. It's a research prompt: it compares Gemini and ChatGPT plans against your own weekly jobs, using only Google's and OpenAI's own pages, and gives you a side-by-side cost table with dates, so you pay for the one that fits. It needs no files and no personal details: describe your jobs in general terms, with no names, emails or account details. Written from Google's and OpenAI's plan, limits, agent and privacy pages, checked 2026-11-21.
Goal: tell me whether Gemini (Google AI plans) or ChatGPT (OpenAI plans) fits my week better, and which plan on that side, from the two companies' own pages only, with every fact dated.
My country: [for example: United States]
What I use now: [nothing / Gemini free / ChatGPT free / a paid plan: which]
My account: [personal / work or school]
My three most common jobs in a week (general terms only, no names or private details):
1. [for example: writing and answering email in Gmail]
2. [for example: summarising long PDFs]
3. [for example: spreadsheets in Excel]
Apps I spend my day in: [for example: Gmail, Google Docs, Word, Excel, PowerPoint]
Storage I need across email, files and photos: [for example: about 100 GB]
Steps, in order:
1. Say in one sentence what you'll do, and that you'll use only these official pages, with a link and today's date for every fact: gemini.google/us/subscriptions (or my country's plans page), support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805 (limits and features), support.google.com/gemini/answer/17094507 (Tasks in Gemini), support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961 (privacy), learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing (ChatGPT prices), chatgpt.com/pricing (ChatGPT features) and help.openai.com (ChatGPT help).
2. If my account is a work or school account, stop and say both companies then apply my organization's terms, and that I should ask whoever runs it.
3. Build a side-by-side cost table, one line per plan, Google on the left and OpenAI on the right, paired by price, not by name: Free with Free, Google AI Plus with ChatGPT Go, Google AI Pro with ChatGPT Plus, Google AI Ultra with ChatGPT Pro. For each: the price as "was … when checked, [today's date]" in my country's currency, the usage limit in that company's words, storage, and whether it includes help inside the apps I named. Write "not shown" for any price or figure a page doesn't show, and never use a third-party price.
4. Warn me, in one line, that the plan names overlap: Google AI Plus and ChatGPT Plus are different prices, and so are Google AI Pro and ChatGPT Pro.
5. For each of my three jobs, say which plans on each side cover it, quoting the line from each company's page that shows it, or "not stated".
6. Say whether each company's agent (Tasks in Gemini; ChatGPT Work) is available on its free version in my country and on which devices, in each company's words.
7. Quote each company's own words on whether my chats are used for training by default and where I change it. If a page doesn't state a default, say "not stated". Never say or suggest either company is safer.
8. Give your suggestion in three to five lines, each tied to a quoted fact, labelled as a suggestion, not either company's advice. Never say "best", "cheapest", "smarter" or "unlimited" unless you're quoting a company, and never use benchmarks.
9. End with a one-week test plan: the same three jobs on both free versions, and what to note (where each one stops me or asks me to wait).
Safety: never ask me for passwords, emails, payment or account details; don't sign up for, start or cancel anything; don't guess anything that isn't on an official page, write "not stated"; date every fact "checked on" today's date; say that prices and features can change and that I should check both pricing pages on the day I buy.
Output: the side-by-side cost table; then the jobs table; then the agents and privacy lines; then "My suggestion"; then the one-week test plan.
