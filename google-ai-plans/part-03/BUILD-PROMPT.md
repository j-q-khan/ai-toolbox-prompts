# Claude build prompt: Google AI Ultra Review: Who Needs the $99.99 or $199.99 Plan?

Part 3 of the Google AI Plans series. The prompt is the script's own "Claude build prompt" section, word for word.

---

Paste this into Claude, in a new chat. It's a research prompt: it checks, from Google's own pages and from what you notice in your own use, whether you'd keep running into Google AI Pro's usage limit, and whether Ultra 5x, Ultra 20x or extra credits would fix it, so you don't pay five or ten times more than you need to. Google publishes no daily counts, so the answer is a reasoned yes, no or "not sure yet", never a number. It needs no files and no personal details: describe your use in general terms, with no names, emails or account details. Written from Google's plans, limits, models and plan-management pages, checked 2026-11-21.
Goal: work out whether I'd keep running into Google AI Pro's usage limit in the Gemini app, and whether Google AI Ultra (5x or 20x) or Google's AI credits would be the better fix, from Google's own pages, with every fact dated.
My country: [for example: United States]
My plan now: [no plan / Google AI Plus / Google AI Pro / Google AI Ultra / not sure]
My account: [personal / work or school]
How often Gemini has told me I'm close to or at my limit in the last month: [for example: never / once or twice / most weeks / most days / I don't know]
What I do in a normal week (general terms only, no names or private details):
[for example: quick questions most days; a long chat about one piece of work; uploading 100-page PDFs; Deep Research reports; making videos in Gemini or Flow; coding with Carbon]
Steps, in order:
1. Say in one sentence what you'll do, and that you'll use only Google's official pages: the plans page for my country (for the US, gemini.google/us/subscriptions), Google One's plan comparison (one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans), "Gemini Apps limits & feature availability" (support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805), "About Gemini models" (support.google.com/googleone/answer/18648722) and "Manage your Google AI plan from Gemini Apps" (support.google.com/gemini/answer/14517446), with a link and today's date for every fact.
2. If my account is a work or school account, stop and tell me Google says models and features then depend on my organization's Workspace license.
3. Quote Google's sentence on how limits work (compute-based, refreshing every 5 hours until a weekly limit) and Google's own table of what uses a little or a lot of usage (simple chat, ongoing conversations, large file analysis, Deep Research, video generation). Say plainly that Google publishes no daily count for any plan, and never invent one, estimate one, or take one from a third party.
4. Sort each thing I do into Google's cost levels (very low, medium or high), quoting the table, and say which of my habits uses the most of a limit.
5. Give my verdict as one of: "Pro is likely enough", "I'd likely run out on Pro", or "not sure yet". Base it only on how often Gemini has told me I'm at my limit and on step 4, with one sentence of reasoning per point. If I said "I don't know", tell me how Google says to check: on a computer at gemini.google.com, Settings at the bottom left, then Usage limits, and to watch for Gemini's limit notifications for two weeks before deciding.
6. Build a table, one line each for Google AI Pro, Ultra 5x and Ultra 20x: price as "was … when checked, <today's date>" in my country's currency (write "not shown" if the page doesn't show it, and never use a third-party price), usage limit in Google's words (say that Ultra's 5x and 20x are against AI Pro, not the free plan), the models in Google's table for chats, storage, and Flow credits.
7. If I'd likely run out only now and then, quote Google's line "You can extend your limits by purchasing AI credits" and tell me to compare the credit price with the jump to Ultra; write "not shown" if no credit price is on an official page.
8. If you suggest Ultra, add three things from Google's pages: Auto picks between Hydrogen and a fast, lightweight model, so Carbon has to be picked by hand at the bottom of the chat box; a hand-picked model defaults to the Low thinking level unless I choose one; and the downgrade path (Google One, Manage membership, Change membership plan, Downgrade options).
Safety: never ask me for passwords, emails, payment or account details; don't sign up for, upgrade, downgrade or cancel anything; don't guess anything that isn't on an official page, write "not stated"; date every fact "checked on" today's date; label your verdict as a suggestion, not Google's advice; never say "best", "cheapest" or "unlimited" unless you're quoting Google.
Output: my verdict in one line; then the cost-level list for my habits; then the plan table; then "If you upgrade" in three lines; then the how-tos, each in one line.
