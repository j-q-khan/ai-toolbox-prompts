### 1. ai-game-maker-checklist.md
```
# The best AI game maker for each kind of game (checklist, facts checked 2026-11-24)

To keep a copy: paste this into Notepad and save it as ai-game-maker-checklist.md with the box called Save as type set to All files. On a Mac: open TextEdit, choose Format, then Make Plain Text, before you paste and save.

Every fact below is from the maker's own page on 2026-11-24. Nothing was signed up for, installed, run or generated. Prices, plans and terms change: check each page before you pay or sell.

## The words
- [ ] No-code maker: a website where you describe your game and it builds it.
- [ ] Coding agent: a helper that writes the code files on your computer.
- [ ] Game engine: the big program games are built in.
- [ ] Credit: a unit your plan gives you; each thing you ask the AI for spends some.
- [ ] Export: turn your game into something others can install or open.
- [ ] Sprite, sprite sheet, tileset, mesh, asset: one flat picture; all its frames on one image; matching squares for a level; a 3D model's surface shape; any ready-made piece of a game.
- [ ] Commercial rights: you're allowed to sell what you make or earn money from it.

## A quick browser game, no code: Rosebud AI (https://rosebud.ai/pricing)
- [ ] Makes "2D games, 3D games powered by Three.js, and voxel worlds", in the browser.
- [ ] Newbie (free): "starts you with 10,000 credits and no credit card, and in eligible regions those credits refill every week".
- [ ] Paid, when checked: Indie Dev $15 a month, 10x Dev $30, Pro $50, Scale $100.
- [ ] Pro and higher: "download your project code and export a Windows build you can publish on Steam".
- [ ] Terms (last revised August 2, 2025): "The free version of the Services is for personal, non-commercial use only." and "UGC created using the paid version of the Services may be used for commercial purposes."
- [ ] Pricing page: "10x Dev and higher plans include full commercial rights". The two pages don't say the same about Indie Dev: check both for your plan.
- [ ] The terms also say: "Rosebud shall own and have all right and title to any data, content, code, artwork, or other materials created or generated as a result of any Prompt." Ask Rosebud what this means for you before you sell.

## An engine on your computer that exports widely: GDevelop (https://gdevelop.io/pricing)
- [ ] "free to use and open-source, under the popular MIT license"; "AI is integrated in GDevelop".
- [ ] "Create your first game 100% free."; Free: "40 free per month" AI credits.
- [ ] Silver, Gold, Pro: prices did not show on the page. The table gives weekly AI credits (200, 1,000, 3,000); the FAQ says the allowance is daily.
- [ ] Publishes to Google Play, Windows, macOS, Linux, the web, gd.games and, on some plans, the App Store.
- [ ] Who owns the games you make: not stated on the pricing page. Check its terms.
- [ ] Its pricing table lists "Sell your games (Steam, App Stores...)" under Monetization, and says a company with more than $50,000 USD revenue a year must use the Pro membership.

## Your own code: Claude Code (https://claude.com/pricing)
- [ ] Included with Claude's paid plans; not on Free. Part 2 of this series builds a browser game with it.

## Every control: Unity or Godot
- [ ] Unity (Unity Technologies): "Build 2D and 3D experiences in any style, for any platform." Its in-editor AI assistant is Part 3.
- [ ] Godot: "Free and open source 2D and 3D game engine" (https://godotengine.org/).

## Art
- [ ] Scenario (https://www.scenario.com/pricing): 2D images and 3D meshes. "All paid plans include a full commercial license." "You own what you create". "Free plan outputs are for personal and evaluation use only." Starter was $15 a month. Free credits: "50 free daily credits" on the plan; the FAQ leaves out "daily".
- [ ] Ludo.ai (https://ludo.ai/pricing): "sprites and animations, icons, UI, textures, music, sound, 3D and video". Indie was $15 a month billed yearly. Terms (latest update 4 November 2026), section 6.3: "You own your Outputs", "for any purpose, personal or commercial", "This applies on every plan, including the free plan." (https://ludo.ai/terms, a PDF)
- [ ] PixelLab (https://www.pixellab.ai/termsofservice): "characters with 4 or 8 directions, animations, tilesets". Terms: "You own the copyrights to your creations, permitting usage for both commercial and non-commercial purposes". Prices didn't load on its page.
- [ ] Meshy (https://docs.meshy.ai/): "Generate 3D assets from images and conversations". Ownership and plans: Meshy AI Part 2 on this channel.

## Before you sell
- [ ] Find the maker's commercial-use line for your plan, in its own terms.
- [ ] Made with a free plan? Check whether the free plan allows selling at all.
- [ ] Keep a copy of the terms page, with the date you checked it.
```
