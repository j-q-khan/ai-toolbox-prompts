# Full code: Gemini Deep Research: Where the Button Went and How to Get a Good Report

A Deep Research prompt template and a source-checking checklist, written from Google's help pages (checked 2026-11-18). They were written from the docs, not run for this video.

### 1. gemini-deep-research-prompt-template.txt
```
# Gemini Deep Research: prompt template (facts checked 2026-11-18)
# Where: gemini.google.com on a computer. In the text box, click Add Files, then Deep Research.
# You need to be 18 or over and signed in. Google lists Deep Research on the Free plan.
# Swap each [bracket] for your own words. Use made-up names, never real people's details.
# To keep a copy: paste this into Notepad and save it with the box called Save as type set to All files.
# On a Mac: open TextEdit, choose Format, then Make Plain Text, before you paste and save.

I'm [what you're doing, e.g. choosing a robot vacuum for a small flat with a cat].
Research [the 3 or 4 things you need, e.g. the main types, what matters for pet hair, typical price ranges].
Focus on [your country or region, and how recent, e.g. the US, from the last 12 months].
Give me [the format, e.g. a short table, then a one-paragraph summary].
List the sources you used, and say where sources disagree.

# After you click Submit, Gemini shows a research plan. Nothing runs yet.
# Missing something? Click Edit plan and say what to change, in plain words.
# Then click Start research. It usually takes about 5-10 minutes (Google). You can leave the chat.
# Your own files only? Add Files, then Files; then Sources, and deselect Google Search.
```

### 2. deep-research-source-checklist.md
```
# Gemini Deep Research: source-checking checklist (facts checked 2026-11-18)

To keep a copy: paste this into Notepad and save it with the box called Save as type set to All files. On a Mac, open TextEdit, choose Format, then Make Plain Text, before you paste and save.
Our advice for checking a finished report. Google's Deep Research help page gives no source-checking steps, so these are ours.

## Before you start
- [ ] 18 or over, signed in at gemini.google.com.
- [ ] In the text box: Add Files, then Deep Research.
- [ ] Look over the research plan before you click Start research (Edit plan to change it).

## When the report is ready
- [ ] Click Open. The report is in the Canvas panel on the right.
- [ ] For every number or claim you'll rely on, open the source it came from.
- [ ] Does that page really say it? Same number, same product, same country?
- [ ] Is the page recent enough? Look for a date.
- [ ] Specs and prices: prefer the maker's own page or an official page.
- [ ] Anything with no source? Treat it as unchecked.
- [ ] Sources disagree? Note both, and check a third.

## Keep it
- [ ] Share & export (on the right in the Canvas panel), then Export to Docs. Google's 2025 tips post says the citations come along in a works cited section.
- [ ] Or Create, then Audio Overview, to listen to it.
- [ ] Find it later under Recent (click Menu at the top if your chats are hidden). Only works if History is on.

## If it seems stuck
- [ ] Google: "It usually takes about 5-10 minutes to generate the report". Longer for complex reports. You can leave the chat.
- [ ] Won't start? Daily research requests and requests at the same time are capped. Gemini says how many are left when you're near the limit.
- [ ] Without a Google AI plan, Deep Research "may be unavailable during periods of high demand" (Google).
- [ ] See your usage: Settings (bottom left), then Usage Limits.
- [ ] Skills don't work with Deep Research yet.

Sources: support.google.com/gemini/answer/15719111 ; support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805 ; support.google.com/gemini/answer/18560919 ; blog.google/products/gemini/tips-how-to-use-deep-research
```
